## Application in the paper

One multivariate DC-VAE model for anomaly detection in mobile ISP monitoring counters/KPIs.

## Reported quantified results

TELCO dataset: 732,607 samples, 7 months, 12 time series, 9,137 anomalies (1.2%), 5-min granularity. DC-VAE mean: recall 67%, precision 47%, F1 52%; median F1 59%.

## Why useful for this literature review

Strong telecom anomaly-detection example using real mobile operational time series and sparse labels.

## Key results to cite

DC-VAE on 12 fixed ISP KPIs. TELCO: ~732k samples, 1.2% NOC labels, Jan–Jul 2021. Test range-F1r: R67%, P47%, F152%, median F159%. ≈S-EXPS/ARIMA; α tuned on labels.

## What to put in your literature review

Core telecom opener: sparse labels, modest F1 on fixed view. Gap: *which* aggregation to score, not *how*. Don't compare range-F1r to your synthetic AUC. Merge #6 for S-VAE only (median F1 31% vs 59%).

## Notes from local PDF

- **Method:** DC-VAE (Dilated Convolutional VAE); single multivariate model over 12 fixed ISP KPIs; causal sliding window (T=512 calibrated on TELCO).
- **TELCO dataset:** ~750k samples, 7 months, 5-min granularity, 12 time series, NOC expert labels at each series; publicly released with code ([https://github.com/GastonGarciaGonzalez/DC-VAE](https://github.com/GastonGarciaGonzalez/DC-VAE)).
- **Evaluation:** Range-based metrics R_r, P_r, F1_r (Tatbul et al.); alpha threshold tuned on training/validation to maximise F1.
- **Benchmarks:** 18 traditional detectors (+ ENS-15 ensemble), S-EXPS, ARIMA, S-VAE; also SWaT with GAN baselines.
- **Authors' finding:** Real in-the-wild telecom detection is much harder than benchmark datasets; F1_r ~60% on 8/12 series; DC-VAE similar on average to S-EXPS/ARIMA but multivariate in one model; S-VAE substantially worse.
- **Thesis gap:** Fixed 12-series view chosen before detection; does not search which aggregation/view to monitor.
